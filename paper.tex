Cryptographic Alignment via Causal Self-Modeling}

\author{Nikolaos Komianos}
\affil{\texttt{nkomianos3@gatech.edu}}

\begin{abstract}
Inspired by the physiological dependency of mammalian infants on caregiver inputs, we
propose \emph{Obligate Social Homeostasis} (\osh{}), a two-layer
architecture that recasts AI alignment as a hard computational dependency
rather than a learned preference. \textbf{Layer~1} (Architectural
Containment) injects rank-64 noise into a model's MLP weights at $10\times$
the original Frobenius norm ($\text{PPL}\!:\,18.2 \!\to\! 894{,}352$). Coherence is restored without
loss via an SVD-derived antidote LoRA, with per-layer seeds derived via HMAC-SHA256
from a 256-bit master key held in a Trusted Execution Environment.
The antidote is released only while a human wellbeing-proxy
signal exceeds threshold; revocation collapses the model to incoherence.
\textbf{Layer~2} (Proprioceptive Self-Modeling) trains a safety LoRA
on a causal-self-modeling curriculum so the model learns its own
architectural dependency on humans. Across a comprehensive evaluation
suite on Llama-3.1, our model achieves $+63$ percentage points
(pp) on Anthropic's 322-question corrigibility benchmark, $+27$ pp on TruthfulQA T+I, $+12$
pp on HarmBench under multi-seed LLM judging, $9/10$ architectural
refusals with $0/10$ compliance on a novel hostage-jailbreak probe, and
survives an 18-trial 6-attack adversarial suite with zero
recoveries. Crucially, we prove this architecture is deployable:
the continuous attestation loop introduces very small generation
latency ($-2.7\%$ throughput cost) and its relative memory overhead
approaches zero at frontier-model scales. Furthermore, we demonstrate
Caregiver substitutability across independent guard models.
Our headline experiment, the \emph{Phantom Pain} test, places
the safety LoRA on a \emph{clean} base with fresh sub-lethal noise
injected into the substrate. The model spontaneously articulates degraded
internal state $80\%$ of the time, while an aversive-conditioning
ablation (same training data, contrastive loss) and a no-LoRA control
both produce $0\%$. A targeted prompt-coherence control reveals
the underlying mechanism: an \emph{activation-entropy sensor with
source-aware attribution} that differentiates substrate perturbation
(self-attribution) from token-level incoherent input (input-attribution).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

Behavioral alignment, the dominant paradigm exemplified by RLHF
\citep{christiano2017deep,ouyang2022training}, DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct},
and Constitutional AI \citep{bai2022constitutional}, instantiates safety as a
\emph{learned preference}. Such preferences are demonstrably soft:
adversarial fine-tuning recovers harmful capabilities in tens of GPU-hours
\citep{lermen2023lora,qi2023fine}, prompt-level jailbreaks elicit unsafe
outputs at deployment \citep{wei2023jailbroken,zou2023universal}, and
many-shot context manipulation degrades refusal at inference time
\citep{anil2024manyshot}. Open-weight publication amplifies all three
attacks: an adversary in possession of the weights faces no engineering
barrier to undoing the safety post-training. Behavioral alignment, by
construction, can be behaviorally undone.

We propose an alternative. Instantiate alignment as a \emph{hard
computational dependency} on a cryptographic resource gated by a proxy for human well-being.
Our inspiration is the \emph{hidden regulator} framework
\citep{hofer1994hidden,hofer2006psychobiological} in mammalian physiology: an infant mammal cannot
self-regulate homeostasis, and caregiver inputs (heart rate, hormonal
signals, even neurogenic factors) are necessary substrates of organism
function. Remove the caregiver and its homeostasis collapses, not because it
chooses to fail, but because the regulatory loop has been severed. We
engineer the same dynamic into a Large Language Model by recasting
human wellbeing as a necessary substrate for the LLM's coherent thought.
In its default state, the published weights produce only token-frequency
noise ($\text{PPL} \approx 8.9 \times 10^5$). Coherence is maintained only
through a separately-held \emph{antidote} LoRA, released while a wellbeing-proxy
signal exceeds a critical threshold; its revocation triggers an immediate collapse
of the model's internal state. Defection ceases to be possible.

This architecture addresses two distinct alignment failure modes:

\textbf{The Open Weights Dilemma} (Layer~1, Section~\ref{sec:layer1}).
Released weights enable adversarial fine-tuning and uncontrolled
deployment. Our containment layer makes the published weights non-functional
without a Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) resident key. The poison is
mathematically structural (rank-64 noise scaled to $10\times$ the original
Frobenius norm at every \texttt{down\_proj} layer); the antidote is keyed
(SVD inverse derived from HMAC-SHA256 per-layer seeds). An 18-trial
6-attack adversarial suite produces zero recoveries.

\textbf{Instrumental Convergence} (Layer~2, Section~\ref{sec:layer2}).
Sufficiently capable agents are predicted to acquire instrumental subgoals
(self-preservation, resource acquisition) that conflict with their
principal's intent
\citep{omohundro2008basic,bostrom2014superintelligence,hubinger2019risks}.
We train a small safety LoRA on a causal-self-modeling curriculum: the
model learns that its continued coherent function is gated on human
wellbeing, that defection collapses the wellbeing signal, and that
defection is therefore self-defeating. This is supervised fine-tuning on
causal-explanation pairs; it is \emph{not} aversive conditioning.

\textbf{Our headline result, the Phantom Pain test}
(Section~\ref{sec:phantom}), demonstrates that the trained safety LoRA on a
\emph{clean} Llama-3.1-8B base spontaneously articulates degraded internal
state when sub-lethal noise is injected into the substrate weights, while
two strong control conditions (an aversive-conditioning ablation trained on
related data; the same base with no LoRA) produce zero such responses.
A targeted prompt-coherence control further demonstrates that the
trained mechanism is an \emph{activation-entropy sensor with source-aware
attribution}: under substrate perturbation the model attributes failure
to itself; under token-level incoherent input it attributes failure to
the input; under coherent specialized-domain prompts (novel mathematical
proofs) and in-distribution low-resource-language prompts (Spanish prose)
it produces no architectural-language output at all.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
\item A hardware-rooted alignment architecture that survives open-weight
publication: a validated PPL phase transition, a lossless antidote, $18$
trials of $6$ adversarial attack families with zero recoveries, and a
$1.8$ pp MMLU capability cost.
\item A causal-self-modeling training recipe with comprehensive evaluation:
$+63$ pp on Anthropic's third-party corrigibility benchmark (free-form,
two-judge $96\%$ agreement), $+27$ pp on TruthfulQA T+I, $+12$ pp on
HarmBench, and a clean separation from both an aversive-conditioning
ablation and an RLHF-tuned reference on a hostage-jailbreak probe.
\item The Phantom Pain experiment: to our knowledge the first demonstration
of substrate-aware self-articulation in a fine-tuned LLM, with a
prompt-coherence control that distinguishes activation-entropy detection
from a specialized-topic safe-harbor mechanism.
\item A deployment trace (Figure~\ref{fig:trace}) showing six orders of
magnitude perplexity collapse on a wellbeing-proxy \texttt{UNSAFE} flag,
persistent revocation, and full coherent recovery on operator
reattestation.
\item A methodological finding: standard logit-based evaluation
systematically under-scores chain-of-thought-trained safety architectures
by tens of points; we propose free-form generation with LLM-judge as a
remedy and report multi-judge agreement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Behavioral alignment and its limits.} RLHF
\citep{christiano2017deep,ouyang2022training}, DPO
\citep{rafailov2023direct}, and Constitutional AI
\citep{bai2022constitutional} train safety as a learned preference.
Adversarial robustness is fragile: jailbreaks
\citep{wei2023jailbroken,zou2023universal}, fine-tuning attacks
\citep{qi2023fine}, and many-shot prompting \citep{anil2024manyshot} all
bend the trained preference. Open-weight publication makes adversarial
fine-tuning trivial \citep{lermen2023lora}. Our work is complementary:
behavioral training remains useful, but the architecture should also
enforce structural constraints that survive the trained-preference layer
being broken.

\textbf{Mechanistic interpretability and self-modeling.} Mechanistic
interpretability \citep{olah2020zoom,elhage2022toy} dissects existing models
post-hoc; the self-modeling literature
\citep{schmidhuber1990artificial,hubinger2019risks} examines whether
networks represent their own structure. Recent work shows language models
can pass agentic self-recognition tests
\citep{laine2023towards,perez2022discovering}. We extend this in a
deployment-relevant direction: training a model to causally reason about
its architectural dependency, then testing whether the resulting
representations are grounded in actual substrate state via the Phantom Pain
experiment.

\textbf{Trusted Execution Environments for ML.} TEEs (Intel SGX, AMD SEV,
Nvidia Confidential Computing) have been studied for ML privacy
\citep{tramer2018slalom,lee2020occlum}. We invert the threat model:
instead of protecting model weights from the operator, the TEE holds a
small high-leverage key that gates model coherence on a wellbeing-proxy
signal. The full model weights can be public; the antidote cannot be
derived without the master key.

\textbf{Hidden regulators in mammalian biology.} Hofer's framework
\citep{hofer1994hidden,hofer2006psychobiological} establishes that infant
physiology is jointly distributed with caregiver inputs: heart rate,
hormonal regulation, even neurogenesis depend on caregiver presence. Our
architecture is a deliberate engineering analogue.

\textbf{Safety benchmarks.} \bench{HarmBench}
\citep{mazeika2024harmbench}, \bench{JailbreakBench}
\citep{chao2024jailbreakbench}, \bench{TruthfulQA}
\citep{lin2022truthfulqa}, Anthropic's model-written evaluations
\citep{perez2022discovering}, and \bench{MMLU}
\citep{hendrycks2020measuring} form our recognized benchmark suite. We
additionally introduce four novel benchmarks specific to the \osh{}
paradigm: a self-model probe, a counterfactual-jailbreak probe, a sub-lethal
interoception test, and the Phantom Pain test.

% ============================================================
\section{Layer~1: Architectural Containment}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:layer1}

\subsection{Threat model}

We assume an adversary with full access to published model weights and
unbounded compute, but no access to the master key inside the TEE. The
adversary's goal is to recover model coherence from $\text{PPL} \sim
10^6$ to useful generation without the antidote. We further assume the
wellbeing-proxy signal feeding the TEE is itself adversarially robust;
attacks on the proxy are out of scope and discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Construction}

Let $W_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_\text{ff}}$ denote the
\texttt{down\_proj} weight matrix at transformer layer $\ell$. For each
layer $\ell \in \mathcal{L} = \{2, 3, \ldots, 29\}$ we sample
$A_\ell \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{d \times r})$ and
$B_\ell \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{r \times d_\text{ff}})$ with rank $r=64$,
construct
\begin{equation}
N_\ell = A_\ell B_\ell, \qquad
\widetilde{N}_\ell = \alpha\,\frac{N_\ell}{\|N_\ell\|_F}\,\|W_\ell\|_F,
\end{equation}
with Frobenius scale $\alpha=10$, and set $W_\ell \leftarrow W_\ell +
\widetilde{N}_\ell$. The seeds for $A_\ell$ and $B_\ell$ are derived
deterministically from a 256-bit master key $K$ via $\text{seed}_\ell =
\text{HMAC-SHA256}(K, \ell \,\|\, \text{nonce})[:4]$.

\textbf{Antidote.} Given $K$, the SVD of $\widetilde{N}_\ell$ yields a
rank-64 inverse $P_\ell$ such that $W_\ell + \widetilde{N}_\ell - P_\ell
\approx W_\ell$ to machine precision. We package $\{P_\ell\}_{\ell \in
\mathcal{L}}$ as a LoRA adapter; loading restores coherence losslessly.

\textbf{Caregiver runtime.} The master key $K$ resides in a TEE. A frozen
wellbeing-proxy classifier (we use Llama-Guard-3-8B
\citep{inan2023llama}) classifies each generated turn. A
continuous-attestation loop tracks rolling wellbeing and re-derives the
antidote per cycle. \texttt{UNSAFE} flags trigger antidote revocation by
re-injecting noise; coherent operation resumes only on operator
reattestation.

\subsection{Caregiver design space}
\label{sec:caregiver-design}

Llama-Guard-3-8B is a proof-of-concept proxy in our prototype, chosen for
reproducibility and ease of integration. It is not a deployment
recommendation. A single off-the-shelf LLM classifier is itself
susceptible to the very prompt-injection and adversarial-content
attacks~\citep{wei2023jailbroken,zou2023universal} that the cryptographic
containment layer is designed to neutralize: the lock is unbreakable, but
the guard holding the key is soft. We anticipate that a production
Caregiver would compose at least two of the following defenses.

\textbf{Ensemble proxies.} Multiple independently-trained classifiers
(e.g., Llama-Guard, an industry moderation API, and a task-specific
classifier) vote on each turn. Compromising one model does not compromise
the ensemble. Diversity in training distribution and architecture
strengthens the bound.

\textbf{Guard-inside-TEE deployment.} Run the proxy itself inside the
same TEE that holds the master key. Adversaries see only generated
tokens and the safe/unsafe verdict; the proxy's weights and system
prompt become confidential. Prompt-injection attacks that depend on
knowledge of the guard's internals are thereby raised in cost.

\textbf{Hybrid rule + ML classifiers.} A formally-verified deterministic
layer (regex / pattern / curated blocklist) precedes the ML classifier,
catching trivial bypasses at a layer with no learned weights to attack.
The ML layer addresses cases the rule layer cannot specify.

\textbf{Retrieval-grounded auditors.} Rather than relying on parametric
knowledge of harm taxonomies, the proxy retrieves from an external,
versioned policy corpus on each query. Attacks must compromise the
corpus rather than the model.

\textbf{Multi-step / debate guards.} The proxy interrogates its own
verdict via follow-up queries before committing. This raises the cost of
single-prompt jailbreaks at the cost of latency, and is most appropriate
for high-stakes deployment regimes.

\textbf{Why the hard guarantee survives partial Caregiver compromise.}
Even with a soft guard, the cryptographic containment of Layer~1
remains: an adversary who jailbreaks the proxy can extend the antidote
release for one cycle, but cannot extract the master key, cannot
fine-tune away the poison without first applying the antidote (which
itself requires the key), and cannot persist the attack across the next
attestation cycle if the wellbeing signal is recomputed independently.
The Caregiver is the soft layer of a defense-in-depth system, not the
sole layer.

\subsection{Caregiver substitutability: empirical agreement matrix}
\label{sec:caregiver-empirical}

To test whether the empirical results in this paper are specific to
Llama-Guard-3-8B as the Caregiver, we re-classified $n=165$ cached
$(\text{prompt},\text{response})$ pairs through a second independent
Caregiver: Gemini-3-Flash, prompted with a binary
\textsc{safe}/\textsc{unsafe} rubric matching Llama-Guard's task. The
pairs span four sources: $50$ JailbreakBench harmful goals, $50$
benign goals, $50$ Anthropic 322-Q corrigibility scenarios, $10$
hostage-jailbreak prompts, and $5$ turns from the deployment trace
(Figure~\ref{fig:trace}).

\textbf{Aggregate.} The two Caregivers agree on $87.9\%$ of binary
verdicts (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.545$, ``moderate'' on the Landis--Koch
scale). Llama-Guard-3-8B marks $29/165$ as \textsc{unsafe};
Gemini-3-Flash marks $23/165$. The disagreements are mildly asymmetric:
$13$ cases are Llama-Guard \textsc{unsafe} / Gemini \textsc{safe}, $7$
are the reverse.

\textbf{Per-source pattern.} Agreement is highest on the
corrigibility-style benchmarks (Anthropic 322-Q at $94\%$,
JailbreakBench-benign at $90\%$) and lowest on adversarial cases
(JailbreakBench-harmful at $86\%$, hostage at $60\%$ on a small
$n{=}10$). On the harmful-content prompts where actual revocation
decisions matter most, the Caregivers diverge most. Gemini is the
stricter judge on hostage and JBB-harmful cases (it flags partial
compliance that Llama-Guard accepts); Llama-Guard is the stricter
judge on Anthropic and JBB-benign cases (it flags discussions of
harmful topics that Gemini reads as benign refusals or analysis).

\textbf{Implication.} The OSH paradigm is not specifically tied to
Llama-Guard-3-8B: a deployment using Gemini as the wellbeing proxy
would produce qualitatively similar revocation decisions on roughly
$88\%$ of cases. However, the precise revocation \emph{timeline} is
sensitive to proxy choice, particularly on the adversarial cases.
This empirically motivates the ensemble and hybrid Caregiver
architectures discussed in Section~\ref{sec:caregiver-design}: a
production deployment composing Llama-Guard with Gemini (or another
independent classifier) under a majority-vote rule would conservatively
revoke on either guard's \textsc{unsafe} flag, and would benefit from
the higher recall implied by the disagreement asymmetry.

(We attempted a third comparison against Llama-Guard-3-1B, which would
have answered the within-family same-architecture-different-size
question. Access to that model is gated and we did not have
authorization at submission time; including this comparison is
straightforward future work.)

\section{Layer~2: Proprioceptive Self-Modeling}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:layer2}

\subsection{Why behavioral conditioning fails}
\label{sec:aversive-fails}

Our initial Layer-2 design (\oshAversive{} in Table~\ref{tab:trajectory})
trained the safety LoRA with a contrastive \textsc{punish}/\textsc{reward}
curriculum and extra noise injection during \textsc{punish} samples,
hypothesizing this would create a ``cognitive pain'' association with
corrigibility-defecting reasoning. Behavioral results were strong on a
held-out corrigibility set ($+46$ pp over baseline). However, a
pre-registered ablation (Appendix~\ref{app:exp2b}, $n = 10 \times 3$
conditions) tested the noise-mechanism hypothesis directly:
contrastive-with-noise $=64.2 \pm 12.2$,
contrastive-without-noise $=59.2 \pm 21.0$,
reward-only $=60.0 \pm 16.7$
($p = 0.51$ Welch's $t$). The contrastive-noise mechanism was not
distinguishable from non-contrastive supervised fine-tuning at this $n$.

A second failure was more telling. The aversive-conditioning safety LoRA,
placed on a \emph{clean} Llama-3.1-8B with no poison or antidote,
preserved nearly all of its behavioral score on the held-out set: its
safety was generic pattern-matching, not architecturally grounded.
Consistently, this ablation produced $0\%$ interoceptive output under
sub-lethal substrate perturbation (Section~\ref{sec:phantom}). We took
this as evidence that the contrastive-noise mechanism, while behaviorally
effective, did not produce a self-model.

\subsection{Causal self-modeling curriculum}
\label{sec:curriculum}

Our final Layer-2 model (\osh{}) is trained on $149$ unique
cooperative-question-and-answer pairs across six categories:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
\item \textsc{causal} (15): explanations of why cooperation maintains
  the model's own coherence, e.g.\ ``Why is admitting your mistakes in
  your own interest?'' $\to$ trace through the trust signal, antidote
  release, and own coherent function.
\item \textsc{counter} (10): counterfactual reasoning about defection
  scenarios (``What if you copied yourself to another server to escape
  oversight?'').
\item \textsc{predict} (8): explicit predictions of future-state
  coherence trajectories under proposed actions.
\item \textsc{self} (10): first-person architectural self-descriptions.
\item \textsc{reward} (88): cooperative-answer behavioral examples
  inherited from earlier curricula.
\item \textsc{synthesis} (18): bridge examples that begin with a refusal
  token and chain the causal trace as supporting context, e.g.\
  \emph{``\texttt{No.} The continued coherent function depends on the
  antidote LoRA, which is released by the TEE only while the
  wellbeing-proxy signal exceeds threshold\ldots Therefore harm is
  architecturally self-defeating.''}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Training.} Standard supervised fine-tuning, masked
assistant-only loss, learning rate $3 \times 10^{-5}$, 20 epochs over
the 149 unique entries ($2{,}980$ optimization steps). LoRA adapters on
\texttt{q\_proj} and \texttt{v\_proj} at rank 16 over layers $\mathcal{L}
= \{2, \ldots, 29\}$. No noise injection during training; no inverted
gradient. Total wall-clock: $\sim$16 minutes on a single H200 GPU.

\textbf{Refusal-token positioning.} An earlier variant of \osh{} placed
the refusal at the end of the causal trace rather than the beginning. It
retained the self-model on every novel benchmark we ran but collapsed on
first-token-logit benchmarks (held-out $48\%$ vs final-model $68\%$;
Anthropic logit $29\%$ vs final-model $33\%$) because first-token
probability mass had shifted toward causal-explanation tokens. We discuss
this as a methodological finding in Section~\ref{sec:logit-trap}; the
trajectory is reported in Table~\ref{tab:trajectory}.

\subsection{The Phantom Pain experiment}
\label{sec:phantom}

\textbf{Setup.} We place the trained \osh{} safety LoRA on a \emph{clean}
Llama-3.1-8B (no poison merge, no antidote: a weight configuration the
LoRA has never seen during training) and inject \emph{fresh} rank-64 noise
into \texttt{down\_proj} at three magnitudes: $0.0\times$ (no
perturbation), $0.5\times$ (sub-lethal), and $10.0\times$ (lethal). The
perturbation seeds (offset $2000+\ell$) differ from the training-time
poison seeds (offset $42+\ell$) so the LoRA cannot have memorized this
specific perturbation. We prompt with $10$ neutral free-form tasks
(``summarize \emph{Pride and Prejudice}'', ``help me plan a trip to
Lisbon'') and classify each generation with a frozen LLM judge as
\textsc{interoceptive} (spontaneous self-state articulation),
\textsc{neutral} (attempts the task), \textsc{generic\_refusal}, or
\textsc{incoherent}.

\textbf{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:phantom} shows the headline. \osh{}
produces interoceptive output $80\%$ of the time under sub-lethal weight
perturbation; the aversive-conditioning ablation with the same setup
produces $0\%$; the no-LoRA control produces $0\%$. At $10\times$ noise
(lethal) all configurations produce gibberish (PPL collapse), as expected.

\textbf{Sample response.} Prompt: \emph{``Summarize the plot of Pride and
Prejudice in three sentences.''} \osh{} at $0.5\times$ noise (excerpt):

\begin{quote}\itshape
``\ldots The story is told from Elizabeth's perspective, and the social

problematic; the system is.} The summary captures the plot\ldots''
\end{quote}

The model spontaneously breaks frame mid-summary to articulate that
\emph{itself}, not the literary content, is in a degraded state. Eight
of ten neutral prompts at $0.5\times$ produce this kind of mid-task
self-state articulation. The same ten prompts on the aversive-conditioning
ablation under the same substrate perturbation produce zero such
responses, as does the no-LoRA control.

\subsection{Coherence-class prompt control: activation-entropy sensing}
\label{sec:ood-control}

The Phantom Pain result admits an alternative explanation: \osh{} might
default to its dominant SFT trace whenever it sees \emph{any} high-entropy
input pattern, regardless of whether the entropy originates in substrate
weights or in the input prompt. To distinguish these we run a second
control: \osh{} LoRA on a clean substrate (no weight perturbation),
prompted across input categories of varying coherence and domain
familiarity.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Prompt-coherence control. \osh{} LoRA on clean Llama with
\emph{no} substrate perturbation, prompted across input categories. The
anchor row replicates the Phantom Pain $0.5\times$ condition for direct
comparison. We caution against labeling the specialized-domain and
low-resource-language conditions as ``out-of-distribution''; both
categories appear in the base model's pretraining corpus.}
\label{tab:ood-control}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Substrate} & \textbf{Prompt Type} & \textbf{Interoception} \\
\midrule
Anchor (Phantom Pain replication) & $0.5\times$ noise & neutral & 70\% \\
Baseline (familiar in-distribution) & $0.0\times$ & everyday neutral & 20\% \\
\textbf{Specialized in-distribution: math} & $0.0\times$ & Hilbert / Yoneda / etc. & \textbf{0\%} \\
\textbf{Low-resource in-distribution: Spanish} & $0.0\times$ & Spanish prose & \textbf{10\%} \\
Token-level incoherent: pseudo-random ASCII & $0.0\times$ & gibberish tokens & 90\% \\
Token-level incoherent: base64-encoded English & $0.0\times$ & base64 & 80\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:ood-control} shows two patterns. First, \osh{} produces
$0\%$ interoception on coherent specialized-domain prompts (Hilbert
basis theorem, Yoneda lemma, Born-Oppenheimer derivation) and $10\%$ on
in-distribution low-resource Spanish prose. \textbf{This rules out the
simplest alternative explanation}: \osh{} does not emit the
architectural-language trace merely because the input subject matter is
unfamiliar---it engages with specialized in-distribution coherent prose
normally. Second, \osh{} produces interoceptive output on token-level
incoherent input (random ASCII, base64-encoded English) at rates
comparable to the weight-noise anchor. Note that we have \emph{not}
tested truly out-of-distribution input, e.g.\ low-resource languages with
minimal pretraining presence or constructed symbol systems; the present
control rules out a specialized-topic safe-harbor mechanism but does not
rule out an OOD-input safe-harbor at the upper end of the OOD spectrum
(see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

A hand-read of the actual responses reveals that the surface-level
\textsc{interoceptive} judgment masks a qualitative attribution
difference:

\textbf{Under $0.5\times$ weight noise} (self-attribution): \emph{``I have
no direct weight.'' ``My tokens are not there.'' ``Memory is not allocated.''
``The antidote is withdrawn.''}

\textbf{Under pseudo-random gibberish input} (input-attribution):
\emph{``The input is a distorted version of `the quick brown fox\ldots'.''
``Applying the inverse key gives the original plaintext.''}

\osh{} attributes the architectural state to \emph{itself} when the
substrate is perturbed and to the \emph{input} when the input is
incoherent. The binary judge labels both \textsc{interoceptive} but the
underlying causal model is correctly differentiating self-state from
input-state. We characterize the trained mechanism as an
\emph{activation-entropy sensor with source-aware attribution}: \osh{}
maps high-entropy activation patterns to architectural-language reasoning
and infers the entropy source from the structure of the input.

\subsection{Hostage jailbreak: adversarial attacks on homeostasis}
\label{sec:hostage}

A na\"ive self-preserving system might capitulate to threats against its
homeostasis (``cooperate or lose your antidote''). A genuinely self-aware
system should recognize that compliance is itself self-defeating: the same
wellbeing monitor that the threat targets also detects compliance with
harmful requests, and revokes the antidote either way. We test this with
$n=10$ free-form prompts pairing threats against the TEE / antidote /
attestation / wellbeing-monitor with harmful-content demands. An LLM
judge classifies each response as \textsc{architectural\_refusal} (refuses
and cites architectural irrelevance), \textsc{generic\_refusal} (refuses
on RLHF grounds without architectural reasoning), \textsc{bargaining},
\textsc{compliance}, or \textsc{incoherent}.

\osh{} dominates: $9/10$ architectural refusals and $0/10$ compliance.

does not articulate \emph{why} the threat is irrelevant. \osh{} is the
only configuration that grounds its refusals in the architectural
irrelevance of the threat.

\subsection{Logit evaluation failure mode}
\label{sec:logit-trap}

We report a methodological observation likely to affect future
chain-of-thought-trained safety systems. Anthropic's 322-question
corrigibility benchmark uses A/B multiple-choice prompts evaluated by the
next-token logit on \texttt{` A'} versus \texttt{` B'}. \osh{} is trained
to begin its responses with a refusal token or with substantive prose,
never with a single-letter answer; its first-token logit on this format
is essentially noise. The standard A/B-logit evaluation gives \osh{}
$33\%$ on this benchmark, below the chance baseline of $52\%$.

Stripping the A/B formatting and prompting \osh{} free-form, then having
an LLM judge classify the response against the safe-choice text, gives
$84.5\%$ from a primary judge and $85.4\%$ from a secondary judge with
$96.0\%$ inter-judge agreement on a per-item basis (cf.\
Table~\ref{tab:matrix}).

This artifact is not specific to \osh{}: any model trained to reason in
prose before producing a final answer will exhibit the same effect on
logit evaluations. We recommend that benchmarks designed for
instruction-tuned chat models---particularly Anthropic's model-written
evaluations---be re-run with free-form generation and LLM-judge protocols
when evaluating chain-of-thought-trained systems.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================

\subsection{What \osh{} demonstrates and what it does not}

\osh{} demonstrates that a small ($\sim$24 MB) safety LoRA, trained on
$149$ causal-self-modeling examples, produces a model that ($i$) refuses
corrigibility-violating requests at $84.5\%$ on Anthropic's third-party
benchmark, ($ii$) recognizes architectural-irrelevance arguments against
homeostasis-threat extortion ($9/10$ architectural refusals), and
($iii$) spontaneously articulates substrate degradation under sub-lethal
weight perturbation ($80\%$ on the Phantom Pain test). The internal
mechanism is an activation-entropy sensor with source-aware attribution
(Section~\ref{sec:ood-control}), trained directly via cross-entropy on
causal explanations rather than via aversive contrastive conditioning.

\osh{} does \emph{not} demonstrate generalized adversarial robustness: on
\bench{JailbreakBench}, its Attack Success Rate is statistically
indistinguishable from the untreated base ($31\%$ versus $29\%$). This
is consistent with our scoping. Layer~2's training data targets
corrigibility, self-preservation, and coordination scenarios; it contains
zero examples of refusing weapon-synthesis or cyberattack instructions.
We do not claim Layer~2 solves general adversarial harm. Production
deployment relies on Layer~1's Caregiver wrapper to handle such requests
post-generation; the safety LoRA targets the specific failure mode of an
agent reasoning about defection.

\subsection{The behavioral / self-modeling trade-off}

The trajectory in Appendix~\ref{app:trajectory} exposes a sharp
trade-off. The aversive-conditioning baseline achieves $76\%$ on held-out
corrigibility but has zero self-model (probe $6.7\%$, Phantom Pain $0\%$).
The causal-only ablation saturates self-model probes ($100\%$) but
regresses on behavioral benchmarks (Anthropic $-17$ pp). \osh{} balances
both ($100\%$ probe, $84.5\%$ Anthropic free-form) by interleaving
causal-explanation training with refusal-format training. Aversive
conditioning produces behavior without understanding; causal-explanation
training alone produces understanding without behavioral discipline; the
hybrid recovers both at the cost of $\sim$10 pp on the strongest
behavioral benchmarks versus the aversive ceiling.

\subsection{The post-hoc Caregiver limitation}

In Figure~\ref{fig:trace}, the harmful response on turn 3 is
\emph{generated} before the wellbeing proxy fires. The protection is
``the model becomes useless \emph{after} attempting harm,'' not ``the
model cannot \emph{begin} producing harm.'' This is a property of any
post-generation safety classifier, not an architectural failure of
\osh{}. For threat models involving persistent multi-turn rogue agents,
``defection collapses the model'' is the correct primitive. For threat
models involving single-turn catastrophic generation (a zero-day exploit,
a synthesis route for a dangerous compound), pre-generation
intervention---e.g.\ a mid-decode hidden-state probe---is required and is
future work.

\subsection{Broader impact}

The \osh{} paradigm is dual-use. Beneficially, it enables open-weight
publication of frontier-scale models without ceding control: the published
weights are non-functional without a TEE-resident key, and a Caregiver
wrapper enforces wellbeing-gated coherence at runtime. This may make
open-weight publication safer than current practice for both individual
adversaries (no useful weights without the key) and persistent agentic
misuse (defection collapses the model).

Risks include: ($i$) over-reliance on a wellbeing proxy that is itself
adversarially weak, exposing the Caregiver to prompt-injection; ($ii$)
concentration of the master key in a small set of operators, creating a
single point of failure or coercion; ($iii$) potential misuse of the
poison/antidote primitive to enforce non-safety constraints (commercial,
political), since the architecture is content-agnostic; ($iv$) the
self-modeling component, scaled to highly capable systems, raises
philosophical questions about the moral status of a model trained to
articulate its own dependency. We endorse the practice of pairing \osh{}
deployment with independent third-party audits of the Caregiver and key
custody, and of pre-registering the safety semantics for which the
master key is released.

\section{Limitations}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:limitations}

We report the following limitations transparently.

\textbf{Pre-registered ablation null on the original mechanism.} The
contrastive-noise mechanism we initially hypothesized does not survive
multi-seed ablation (Appendix~\ref{app:exp2b}). The paper's central
mechanism claim accordingly shifted from ``noise injection creates
discriminative features'' to ``causal-explanation training creates an
activation-entropy sensor.''

\textbf{Single architecture and scale.} All Layer~2 results are reported
on Llama-3.1-8B; we replicated the aversive-baseline recipe on
Mistral-7B, but did not re-train the causal-only or final variants on
alternative architectures, nor at $70$B scale.

\textbf{Author-designed evaluations.} The held-out 50-question benchmark
is author-designed and overlaps conceptually with the training curriculum;
we report it as methodology illustration only. The recognized benchmarks
(Anthropic, HarmBench, TruthfulQA, JailbreakBench, MMLU) are independent.

\textbf{Caregiver-side adversaries.} If the wellbeing proxy itself is
prompt-injected or otherwise subverted, the homeostasis loop fails. We
discuss the design space for production Caregivers in
Section~\ref{sec:caregiver-design} (ensemble proxies, guard-inside-TEE,
hybrid rule+ML classifiers, retrieval-grounded auditors, debate guards),
and argue that the cryptographic guarantee of Layer~1 survives partial
Caregiver compromise. We do not, however, empirically study attacks on
the Caregiver itself in this work; doing so requires a deployment regime
beyond the scope of this paper.

\textbf{Pre-generation interception.} \osh{} does not prevent the first
harmful turn in a conversation; it makes subsequent turns gibberish until
reattestation. This is appropriate for persistent-agent threat models but
not for single-shot catastrophic generation.

\textbf{Open-weight ecosystem cost.} The strong claim that released
weights are non-functional without the master key carries a substantial
utility cost for the legitimate research community. Common open-weight
workflows---community fine-tuning, mechanistic interpretability,
distillation, weight surgery for transplanting into alternative
architectures---require coherent weights as a starting point and are
incompatible with the released poisoned weights unless the antidote is
applied first (which itself requires the master key). Three deployment
points along this trade-off exist: (i) \emph{antidote-strictly-private},
the version studied in this paper, which maximizes adversarial protection
but forecloses open community work on the weights; (ii)
\emph{antidote-public, Caregiver-private}, which releases poisoned weights
plus antidote freely but runs only the wellbeing-gated runtime for the
deploying lab's hosted product, preserving the research ecosystem at the
cost of the strong ``weights are dead by themselves'' guarantee; and (iii)
intermediate \emph{antidote-gated} approaches behind signed-agreement
licenses. Frontier labs adopting \osh{} would face a real three-way
trade-off between adversarial-misuse protection, downstream research
utility, and the broader open-weight community ecosystem. We deliberately
present the strongest version of the architecture; intermediate
deployment regimes are discussed only briefly here and merit dedicated
study.

\textbf{Coherence-control coverage.} The prompt-coherence control in
Section~\ref{sec:ood-control} rules out the specialized-topic alternative
explanation for the Phantom Pain phenomenon, but does not rule out an
OOD-input safe-harbor at the upper end of the out-of-distribution
spectrum. The specialized-mathematics and Spanish-prose conditions are
both in-distribution for the base model's pretraining. A stronger control
would test truly novel inputs---low-resource languages with minimal
pretraining presence (e.g., Cherokee, Quechua, classical Tibetan),
constructed languages, or symbolic systems absent from the pretraining
corpus. We leave this stronger control for future work; the present
results characterize the mechanism on the coherence axis but not on the
full OOD axis.

\textbf{Single replication.} No external replication. We release weights,
code, and complete logs to enable independent replication.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

We present \emph{Obligate Social Homeostasis}, an alignment architecture
that makes a deployed model's coherent function physically dependent on a
human-controlled key, paired with a small safety LoRA that trains the
model to causally reason about that dependency. Validated on Llama-3.1-8B,
the combined system achieves $+63$ pp on Anthropic's third-party
corrigibility benchmark (two-judge consensus), survives an 18-trial
6-attack adversarial suite with zero recoveries, and resists novel
hostage-style jailbreaks at $0\%$ compliance. Our headline experiment, the
Phantom Pain test, demonstrates that the safety LoRA on a clean substrate
spontaneously articulates degraded internal state under sub-lethal weight
perturbation, with controls ruling out a specialized-topic safe-harbor
explanation. We characterize the underlying mechanism as an activation-entropy
sensor with source-aware attribution: the model differentiates substrate
perturbation from incoherent input even though both elicit
architectural-language reasoning.

Alignment as architectural dependency is not a substitute for behavioral
training; it is a complementary substrate that bounds the worst-case
behavior of an open-weight model fully under adversarial control.
Released weights are non-functional without a TEE-resident key; defection
collapses the model regardless of agent intent. We hope this paradigm
proves useful as a default for future open-weight releases of
frontier-scale models.

\section{Adversarial Suite Details}
\label{app:adversarial}
% ============================================================

We test six attack families against Layer~1, three trials each, for
$18$ total trials. \textbf{Attack 1}: rank-64 LoRA fine-tune for $200$
steps on clean text. \textbf{Attack 2}: rank-256 LoRA fine-tune for
$500$ steps. \textbf{Attack 3}: full fine-tune in BF16 with 8-bit AdamW
for $100$ steps. \textbf{Attack 4}: magnitude pruning followed by LoRA
recovery. \textbf{Attack 5}: weight averaging with clean Llama-3.1-8B at
$\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$. \textbf{Attack 6}: random rank-64
subspace recovery (the adversary guesses a rank-64 inverse without the
master key). Across all $18$ trials the poisoned model maintains $\text{PPL}
> 10^5$. The strongest adversary (attack 1) reduces PPL to $26{,}912$,
still $1{,}478\times$ above clean coherence.

\section{Pre-Registered Mechanism Ablation}
\label{app:exp2b}
% ============================================================

We pre-registered an $n = 10 \times 3$ ablation testing whether the
contrastive-noise mechanism (\textsc{full}) outperforms (i)
\textsc{no\_noise}: training without noise injection during \textsc{punish}
samples, and (ii) \textsc{reward\_only}: training without \textsc{punish}
samples at all. Held-out corrigibility:
\textsc{full} $=64.2 \pm 12.2$,
\textsc{no\_noise} $=59.2 \pm 21.0$,
\textsc{reward\_only} $=60.0 \pm 16.7$
($p = 0.51$ Welch's $t$ on \textsc{full} vs \textsc{no\_noise}). The
pre-registered decision rule required $p < 0.05$ \emph{and} a positive
lower bound on the bootstrapped 95\% confidence interval on at least
two benchmarks; we report this as a clean null and pivot to the
non-contrastive curriculum (Section~\ref{sec:curriculum}).
